## Architecture and financial authority

`CashierStore` restores the authenticated cashier's own open shift through `get_open_cashier_shift`; `start_cashier_shift` records opening cash and terminal. The local Cashier state associates transactions with the shift and computes an informational open-shift estimate. GCash collections are separate from physical Cash. The authenticated `close_cashier_shift` RPC locks the cashier's open shift, rejects unresolved restaurant-wide online GCash payments, validates a nonnegative count and variance reason, rounds to cents, computes its own verified Cash amount, and stores expected/count/variance with timestamps and attribution. The UI must treat that RPC result as final. `get_closed_cashier_shifts` reads saved snapshots with date filters and keyset pagination; historical figures are not recomputed from today's transaction list. No duration ceiling or mandatory denomination count exists in the inspected contract.

The manual ₱4,000 opening, ₱5,420/₱5,585 expected and September closed-shift figures are prior observations, not live data checks. No hosted shift was closed, reopened, or edited during this work.

## Lifecycle, findings, and implementation

The active cashier starts their own shift with opening cash and a terminal, takes sales, then reviews an informational drawer estimate and counts physical cash. Pending online GCash anywhere in the restaurant blocks closure. The cashier supplies a reason for an estimated difference and confirms; the server rechecks its rules, writes the settlement once, and the Cashier can read the saved closed row. Server authorization and shift ownership, not a client terminal choice, govern opening and closing. The existing 12-hour UI warning identifies a long-running shift without closing it. The backend has no shift-age limit, and duration spans calendar dates without resetting shift identity.

The reviewed closure dialog now labels open figures and variance as estimates, explains opening cash plus completed cash sales less client-classified cash refunds, and keeps GCash outside the drawer. It exposes the variance reason even when the browser estimate balances so a server-side difference can be explained on retry. It enforces finite, nonnegative counts with at most two decimal places and shows specific errors; variance display rounds to cents. The review is invalidated when shift identity, estimated expected cash, or pending-payment status changes. A synchronous submission guard prevents double clicks; the confirmation remains open through the request and displays a rejected RPC error. Both Shift Settlement and Dashboard pass the active shift identity and propagate closure failure to the dialog. Dashboard success no longer claims the drawer is balanced from the client estimate; it points to the server-saved settlement.

`ClosedShiftHistory` was inspected but not edited: it reads saved expected/count/variance and attribution via `get_closed_cashier_shifts`, validates date range, and pages 25 rows. No exact as-closed cash/GCash breakdown is stored, so linking a historical row to today's mutable receipts would not prove what was counted then. The Manager financial report uses verified fulfilled revenue by fulfillment date, which is a different measure from shift collections by `shift_id`. The prior [Manager–Cashier reconciliation](MANAGER-CASHIER-FINANCIAL-RECONCILIATION-202610.md) confirms this separation, but its October 23 hosted totals are historical evidence only. The active refund mapper currently maps hosted cashier transactions to Completed or Voided; the `Refunded` branch in local shift calculation is therefore a legacy/client-state risk rather than a demonstrated live refund error. No hosted refund scenario was run.

## Risks and deferred decisions

The browser estimate and server closure formula may disagree for refunds, void/reverification state, or stale local records. A zero estimated variance can still be nonzero at server closure; the RPC rejects a missing reason and the dialog now allows recovery, but the exact final amount is only available after the server commits. Aligning these financial definitions requires separate backend/financial authorization and isolated regression, including Manager reporting and paid cancellation. No formula was changed here. Restaurant policy is needed before adding a maximum shift duration, variance tolerance, mandatory denomination count, or additional closure prerequisites. Existing long-shift warning and restaurant-wide pending-payment guard remain. Hosted snapshot immutability and authenticated history behavior retain the limitations recorded in the prior financial reconciliation; this task did not change them.
