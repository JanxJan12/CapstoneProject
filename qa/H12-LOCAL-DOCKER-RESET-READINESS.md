## Evidence required before each retirement decision

For **each exact stack**, record a dated owner/custodian decision, last-use evidence, active browser/test/job/client review, role and dependency map, live Compose/Supabase labels, full container IDs, images/digests, restart policies, networks, port bindings, environment/config references without printing secrets, every named/bind mount and its owner, original running state, and DB/Storage health. Record which artifacts and test results depend on the fixture and their retention location. Do not infer idleness from age, low CPU or a label alone.

Before **container deletion**, preserve an exact recreation manifest and prove a controlled start from the same retained DB/Storage volumes in a separate authorized trial. Verify Auth, REST, read-only SQL, Storage access, needed feature services, original ports, and rollback timing; ensure no duplicate port listener. Deleting containers loses configuration and process state even when volumes remain. A stack with unique evidence stays protected until its evidence is independently retained and reviewed.

Before **volume deletion**, require a per-stack, independently recoverable set: PostgreSQL roles/schema/data and managed Auth/Storage metadata; migration ledger; RLS policies, grants, triggers, functions, extensions and bucket settings; all private Storage object **bytes** with per-object size and SHA-256; relevant configs, synthetic fixture provenance and source hashes. Put the manifest and archive in a restricted client-owned location outside this checkout, use authenticated encryption and an owner-held recovery key, copy to an independent off-device destination, verify archive hashes **from that copy**, extract it, and restore into a newly named unlinked stack. Compare counts, foreign keys, catalog fingerprints, migration versions, Auth sign-in, role denials, private bucket access, object hashes and representative RRJ workflows; record elapsed restore time and failures. Test key recovery without exposing credentials. Ordinary SQL dumps, Docker volume existence, a same-drive copy, a stopped container, and synthetic one-object drills do not meet this gate. For a truly disposable fixture, a separately signed retention/discard decision must explicitly identify what unique data/evidence is being surrendered; it cannot be inferred from the word “synthetic.” Protected demo, recovery, `isolated-stack`, todo and unknown volumes are excluded from any general RRJ retirement.
