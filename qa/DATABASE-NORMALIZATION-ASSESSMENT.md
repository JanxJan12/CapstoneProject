# Database normalization assessment — 2026-10-14

**Scope:** 34 `public` tables. Read-only, source/catalog assessment. No hosted connection or database modification was made in this pass. The [existing detailed matrix](DATABASE-NORMALIZATION-PERFORMANCE-ASSESSMENT-20261014.md) supplies the full FD narrative; this deliverable adds explicit BCNF, purpose, evidence confidence, and action for every table. The [disposition JSON](DATABASE-DISPOSITION-REGISTER-20261014.json) supplies complete constraint definitions and 378 column types.

## Method and rating

`K` and `AK` below mean declared primary and alternate uniqueness only; nullable unique columns are keys only in their non-null subset. `Y` means no contrary dependency was identified, **not formal proof**. `C` means JSON/array or snapshot semantics need business-grain verification. `D` means a stored derivation/projection creates a maintenance dependency. For BCNF, `E` is an evidenced nonkey arithmetic determinant; `P` is a possible nonkey determinant needing business-rule and data checks; `U` means none identified and BCNF cannot be proven from present evidence. Candidate-key completeness and all semantic FDs cannot be established from DDL alone. “High” confidence is structural catalog evidence; “Medium” is an inferred business rule; no row-level claim is made.

## Normal-form conclusions

- **1NF:** Conventional scalar columns and child tables handle most repeating data. The array/JSON columns (`menu_items.aliases`, `order_items.modifiers`, `inventory_opening_balances.request_payload`, `inventory_request_documents.issue_input`, `inventory_request_revisions.snapshot`, `inventory_request_events.details`) are deliberate search, event, or immutable-document payloads. Their element-level relational 1NF is conditional; inspect only if elements need independent constraints or joins.
- **2NF:** Composite PK bridges (`inventory_request_lines`, `inventory_request_revisions`, `menu_item_modifier_groups`) have whole-row pair/version grains. Request-line names/unit/scale could look partially dependent on inventory item, but they capture the issued document time; confirm immutability and lineage before calling this a violation. Surrogate IDs alone prove nothing about other candidate-key dependencies.
- **3NF/BCNF:** Checked `order_items.line_total = unit_price × quantity`, `inventory_transactions.quantity_after = quantity_before + quantity_change`, and the `orders.grand_total` arithmetic are explicit nonkey determinants. They are purposeful financial or audit projections. They prevent a blanket strict 3NF/BCNF claim; preserve them with transactional writers and reconciliation. Other apparent determinants (payment → order, batch → item, PO line → item, receipt → source) cross tables and are **potential consistency anomalies**, not proven in-table normal-form violations.
- **Confirmed object redundancy:** the prior audit found duplicate timestamp triggers on `profiles` and `riders`; guarded local removal was already prepared and tested. This is not a redundant table/column/history row. No new confirmed redundant table, index, or business row emerged.

## Evidence and limits

The [architecture report](DATABASE-ARCHITECTURE-OPTIMIZATION-20261014.md) and [ERD](DATABASE-TARGET-ERD-20261014.mmd) establish authority and link meaning. The [redundancy audit](DATABASE-REDUNDANCY-AUDIT-20261013.md) found 21 repeated order-status events with differing notes/timing; `(order_id,status)` is not a key. The hosted catalog snapshot gives declared constraints, not complete semantic rules, external consumers, or proof of absence of anomalous rows. The nine empty hosted tables are conditional/historical contracts. No data, schema, or migration was changed.
